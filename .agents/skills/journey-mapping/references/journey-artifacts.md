# Journey artifacts and evidence

## Select the artifact

| Artifact | Main question | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Journey map | How does a person pursue a broader goal over time and touchpoints? | Wider context, channels, needs, breaks, and outcomes |
| User flow | What steps, branches, and decisions occur within a product task? | More detailed interaction path; not a cross-channel journey |
| Service blueprint | How do frontstage interactions connect to backstage people, systems, and processes? | Service operations and handoffs; requires evidence about delivery |
| Empathy map | What is known or assumed about a user/segment's perspective? | Organizes evidence; does not replace research or constitute a persona |
| Story map | What activities and stories support shared understanding and product planning? | Product delivery/discovery planning; not interchangeable with journey mapping |

## Evidence rules

- State actor, goal, scope, dates/source, and channels covered.
- Use exact evidence when supplied; label reported statements and inferred emotions separately.
- Hypothesis-first maps are allowed for planning, but mark every unsupported stage and plan user research to validate or revise it.
- Do not treat an empathy map as observed fact, a journey map as causal proof, or a user flow as a full service account.
- Use a participant/sample description only when supplied; do not fabricate sample size or representativeness.
- Prioritize one opportunity with evidence, impact rationale, confidence, owner/next step, and a falsification test.
