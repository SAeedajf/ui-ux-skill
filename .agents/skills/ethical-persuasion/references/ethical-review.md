# Ethical persuasion review

## Decision checks

- **User benefit:** What current, traceable evidence from affected users shows this behavior is useful? Is user value clearly separated from revenue/retention goals, and is doing nothing an available choice? Do not infer user value from conversion alone.
- **Truth:** Are claims, scarcity, social proof, prices, renewals, and consequences accurate and verifiable?
- **Agency:** Can users compare, decline, defer, undo, opt out, downgrade, or cancel without punishment or unnecessary friction?
- **Proportionality:** Is the intervention proportionate to the benefit, and does it avoid exploiting vulnerability or urgency?
- **Evidence:** Are observed behavior, user reports, causal evidence, and hypotheses distinguished?
- **Outcomes:** Measure comprehension and task success with conversion; inspect complaints, refunds, accidental purchases, renewal outcomes, and exit success over a follow-up window covering the refund/cancellation period and relevant renewal; for annual plans, follow through the first annual renewal where feasible. Predefine decision criteria, guardrail thresholds, and stop/remediation actions; report affected groups. If renewal follow-up is infeasible, disclose the limit and do not treat short-term conversion as user-benefit evidence.

## Safer substitutions

- Replace a resetting countdown with a real, dated offer window or remove the timer.
- Replace a hidden plan with a visible comparison and a plain explanation of differences.
- Replace obstructed cancellation with a direct, accessible cancellation path on the signup channel where feasible, no retention gate, and clear confirmation.
- Replace pressure prompts with a dismissible, frequency-limited reminder that respects the user's choice.

## Source boundaries

This Skill synthesizes guidance from named sources; it does not reproduce their articles, books, case-study graphics, or proprietary templates. Registry entries identify source type and access scope. Behavioral models and commercial case studies help generate questions; they do not establish universal effects or guarantee conversion. For claim-level citations, consult the reviewed source directly and cite the specific page or study.
