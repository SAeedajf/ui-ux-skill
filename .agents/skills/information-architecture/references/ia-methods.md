# Information architecture methods

## Match method to question

| Question | Useful method | What it can support |
|---|---|---|
| What content exists and what is duplicated or missing? | Content inventory/audit | A documented inventory and candidate cleanup |
| How do people group topics or interpret labels? | Card sorting (open, closed, or hybrid) | Patterns in participant grouping/terminology; not findability by itself |
| Can people find a target in a proposed hierarchy? | Tree testing | Task success and path issues for the tested tree; not visual menu usability or preference |
| Where do people expect to find a feature during a real task? | Task observation/contextual inquiry | Observed behavior in the tested context; not universal preference |

## Evidence discipline

- Identify target audience and recruitment limitations before calling results representative.
- Keep task wording neutral and avoid category names that reveal the answer in a tree test.
- Preserve raw response counts and task wording when available; do not invent sample sizes or convert a small directional study into population certainty.
- Distinguish current product analytics, observed behavior, participant-reported language, and a proposed taxonomy.
- Compare meaningful alternatives and state the smallest follow-up test that could change the recommendation.

## Source boundaries

This independently written guide synthesizes method distinctions; it does not copy paid research, source prose, or proprietary templates. Registry records identify access and evidence limitations. Tool-provider pages describe available research methods, not proof that a particular taxonomy works.
